Student toolkit

Practical methods for learning and thinking. Use what helps; ignore what doesn't.

01 / LEARNING

Active recall

Retrieve information from memory before looking at the answer.

  • Read a small section.
  • Close the source and explain it in your own words.
  • Check what you missed, then retry later.
02 / REASONING

Think in steps

Make your reasoning visible so you can test it.

  • State the question precisely.
  • Separate known facts from assumptions.
  • Write the next step and why it follows.
03 / FOCUS

Single-task session

Reduce friction before starting instead of relying on motivation.

  • Choose one finishable task.
  • Put distractions out of reach.
  • Work for 20–30 minutes, then take a short break.
04 / REVIEW

Weekly reset

Use evidence from the week to adjust your plan.

  • What actually moved forward?
  • What created unnecessary friction?
  • What is one realistic priority for next week?
05 / MEMORY

Spaced practice

Revisit important ideas over time, not just once in a long session.

  • Review after a day.
  • Review again a few days later.
  • Spend more time on what you cannot recall.
06 / WELLBEING

Protect the basics

Learning works better when your routine is sustainable.

  • Keep sleep and breaks realistic.
  • Leave room for friends and family.
  • Adjust the plan when your workload changes.
